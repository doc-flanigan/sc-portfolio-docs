Flipped 2026-09-16 (supersedes current-alpha-version-4-9-0, which is retained as history
because "did 4.9 wipe" answers still render). Released **2026-08-26**, build
**4.10.0-LIVE.12519617**. Note this shipped ~5 weeks after entering PTU on 2026-07-23 —
an unusually long PTU that CIG said was deliberate, so the site copy asserting "no release
date announced" went stale for **20 days** before this flip. The auto-filed release-pass
issue (SCH #56, opened 2026-08-31) is the signal to act on next time.

Headline content: **Instancing** arrives in the Persistent Universe (private per-party
copies of a location, party leader owns the instance, new Instancing Broker backend
service); **Siege of Orison V2** as the first Instancing-on-Demand mission (closed
instance for up to four players, pure FPS, unlimited medical-bed checkpoints that do not
consume MedGel); **Kruger S-65 Stingray**, Kruger's first medium fighter; hydrogen +
quantum **fuel rebalance** (smaller hydrogen tanks, reduced consumption, substantially
higher fuel prices); **ASOP claim rework** (reclaim timers scale with vehicle value,
fitted weapons priced into claim cost, paid expedite capped at a tenth of the standard
rate); S4 ballistic gatlings ~68% harder per round; vehicle armor no longer mitigates
damage at zero health; ordnance up 44–310%.

**WIPE = NO.** Handle this carefully: the release notes contain **no wipe statement at
all** — the positive evidence is that LTP was *extended*, with ore and commodity
containers added to Long Term Persistence (not retroactive; cargo obtained from 4.10
onward persists into future builds, and ship-supplied ore pods remain item recovery
rather than LTP). Per [[feedback_fact_check_absence]] absence is not refutation, so do
not paraphrase this as "the patch notes state LTP was preserved" the way the 4.9 claim
legitimately could — 4.9 had an explicit LTP-preserved line and 4.10 does not.

TRAP (carried over from 4.9, still live): CIG's abridged Spectrum posts reuse earlier
template numbers. The 479 bug fixes / 47 crash-and-stability / 17 exploits figures used
here come from the full release notes with a per-category crash breakdown, which is why
they were judged genuine — but re-derive them rather than copying if they ever need
restating.
